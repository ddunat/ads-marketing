The month lost money on first purchase. 12 won jobs produced $4,669 of gross profit against $6,919 of ad spend — a $2,250 shortfall. It only reaches break-even if the 20 leads still open close at the rate the resolved ones did.

Of the 72 conversions Google counted, 40 were form submissions, 18 were calls placed from the ad and 14 were taps on the phone number on your site. The CRM holds 43 ads opportunities from the month — more than the forms Google counted. Cost per enquiry you can actually work: $161.

The calls make it less bad, not good enough. They move the gate from 0.70 to about 1.61 — real, and worth having. They do not get you to scale.

Clearing 3:1 on September's spend takes 51 won jobs. Google counted 72 conversions, number taps included, so you would need 71% of everything it recorded to become paid work. The cohort's actual win rate is 27.9%. That last row clears the gate only by assuming a conversion rate two and a half times what the month delivered.

The counting runs the other way too: the CRM holds 43 ads opportunities against the 40 form submissions Google recorded, so Google is under-counting forms, not inflating them. Cost per enquiry you can actually work is $161.

Better before More. Volume is not the constraint — you are already buying 43 leads a month. The constraint is that a $620 inspection carries $389 of gross profit, and no amount of ad optimisation changes that arithmetic.

The one lever with 2.3× in it is average job value. A construction-stage package sells at $3,095 against $620 for a pre-purchase inspection. Converting even a small share of ads leads into packages moves the gate in a way that cutting cost per click never will.
